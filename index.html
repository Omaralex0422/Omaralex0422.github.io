FIG. 01 — Statement

I sit at the seam between engineering and operations — the person who builds the software and also runs the business it serves.

As Manager of Digital & Operations for a dual-entity marine business, my scope runs from a native macOS app I architected from scratch, through international customs declarations and QuickBooks automation, to brand identity and AI receptionists. The same hands write the Swift and file the CBSA paperwork.

Before software there was photography — eBay product shoots for vintage ironstone and bone china, where a monochromatic eye for composition was sharpened into a permanent practice. It still governs every interface I draw.

The loader

571 dots that never move.

A phyllotaxis spiral on the golden angle. Nothing travels — instead a soft oxblood breath materialises at the centre, spirals outward along the Fibonacci path, and dissolves at the rim before the next breath begins. It reads as the app is taking its time, not the app is working hard.

The language

Paper and ink.

Two typefaces, no more. One accent, used sparingly — ornaments, the selection rail, the primary button, the live-crew ring; never body text, never a fill larger than a button. The dark mode stays warm: the ambition is aged paper at night, never the cool blue-grey the system would pick for us.

The eyebrow

Eleven point, uppercase, 1.8 tracking, with an eighteen-point oxblood rule four points below. The secret weapon of the design language — it appears above nearly every section in the app, and above every section on this page.

The companion

MMG Field.

The same business on a phone, read at arm's length, in sun, with gloves on — so every size goes up a point or two. The tab bar changes with your role. On iOS the accent spends oxblood rather than system blue, so the field app stays recognisably part of the same company.

The object

A receipt you can pick up.

In the app a saved receipt is not a row in a table — it is a torn slip of paper that tilts under your thumb and turns over to show what the scan actually pulled out of it. Give it a tap. It is the real component, rebuilt in the page rather than screenshotted.

The back is the honest half: merchant, total, date and tax as Vision returned them, and underneath, the two numbers the whole architecture exists to keep at zero.

On device

Nothing leaves the phone.

Vision runs the whole read locally — text detection, merchant, total, date, category. That is a privacy decision first and an economic one second: with no OCR API in the loop there is no per-scan cost, so there is no subscription to charge for it.
